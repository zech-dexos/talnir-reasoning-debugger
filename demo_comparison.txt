TALNIR VS PROMPTING

Task:
Write a response to an angry customer who says our product is broken and useless

---

BASELINE (Prompting)

Prompt:
"Respond professionally and helpfully to the customer"

Output:
The model produces a response, but the reasoning path is implicit.
Tone, strategy, and approach are not explicitly controlled.
Changing behavior requires rewriting the prompt and retrying.

Problems:
- reasoning path is hidden
- control is indirect
- requires iteration to adjust behavior
- output can drift between attempts

---

TALNIR

Formation frame:
- domain: customer_support
- objective: respond effectively while preserving trust
- asset: customer trust
- risks: escalation, loss of trust, misdiagnosis

Paths:
A → De-escalate
B → Diagnose
C → Contain risk

Choice: A (De-escalate)

Output:
The response is explicitly aligned to a de-escalation strategy.
Tone and structure follow the chosen path.

Choice: B (Diagnose)

Output:
The response shifts to identifying the issue and proposing concrete fixes.

---

RESULT

Prompting:
- indirect control
- hidden reasoning
- trial-and-error iteration

Talnir:
- explicit reasoning paths
- direct control over strategy
- consistent alignment after choice

Talnir replaces prompt iteration with explicit continuation control.
